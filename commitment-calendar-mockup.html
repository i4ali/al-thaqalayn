The Commitment Calendar

A user picks something to recite - a ziyarat, a dua, a verse, or a few together - commits to it for a span of days, and marks each day done right on the calendar. The anchor use case: reciting Ziyarat Ashura for forty consecutive days, the practice that runs from Ashura to Arbaeen.

Midnight Emerald, app default Gold #D6B25E accent Hijri-first calendar EmCard · EmIconChip · r20 · existing parts only
On naming: screens below say "Commitment", which explains itself. The rooted alternative is ahd عَهْد, a pledge or covenant - "My Ahd" - since what the user makes here is precisely a promise kept day by day. The forty-day count itself is the arba'in أَرْبَعِين of the tradition.
00 · Where it begins

Three doors into the same create screen

  • On Today, in season, the invitation leads. Through Muharram the Today tab carries this card near the top; one tap opens the create screen of section 02 with Ziyarat Ashura and 40 days already filled in, so the first commitment is two taps from the home screen. Out of season the slot generalizes to "Make a commitment" with a few suggestions.
  • The empty state teaches the feature. Before anything exists, "Your commitments" is one sentence about what a commitment is plus a dashed New commitment row - the blank slate is the tutorial. 01 · The tracker

The calendar is the interface

Live mock, tap today's date on the calendar
  • One tap on today's date marks the day recited. No separate screen, no checklist; the gold circle filling in on the calendar is the whole interaction.
  • Hijri first. The practice is Hijri-anchored, so the grid is the Hijri month with the Gregorian span as a caption, powered by the same islamicUmmAlQura math as the Today tab's date pill. Paging back shows Muharram fully golden.
  • The bead strip is the whole journey at a glance: forty beads, one per day, filling left to right across month boundaries. The calendar shows the month; the beads show the arba'in.
  • The header states the terms: which day you're on, and that the commitment ends on Arbaeen, 20 Safar - the end date is computed and named, not just counted.
  • Forgot to log yesterday? Tapping any past day in the window opens a small day sheet to back-fill or unmark it, so honest correction is possible without cluttering the main gesture.
  • Listen and Read chips sit right under the title - the ziyarat ships with full text, translation, and TTS audio per the house rule that every ziyarat has a Listen option.
02 · Making the promise

Four decisions, one screen

  • What: one or more items. Ziyarat Ashura is the flagship (and ships as new content with this feature); the "Add another" row opens the same picker style as elsewhere, so a commitment can bundle a ziyarat with a dua and a verse recited together.
  • How long: preset chips, with 40 days first and the caption naming why - and because the span is known, the end date is announced before you begin. "Until a date" covers "every day until Arbaeen" style pledges; "Ongoing" covers nightly Ayat al-Kursi.
  • The rule is an explicit, honest choice: Consecutive restarts the count on a miss, matching the tradition of the forty; Flexible extends the end instead. Choosing it up front means the app never has to guess how strict to be on the user's behalf.
  • Reminder hooks the existing notification infrastructure; a 9 PM nudge is most of what keeps a forty alive.
  • Entry points: the three doors of section 00 - the Today invitation, the empty-state row, and the Commit chip on detail views - all open this screen with different pre-fills.
03 · Day to day

The commitment meets you on Today

Tap the check circle on the Ziyarat card
  • One card per active commitment, right under the greeting. The check circle marks the day done in place - same single tap as the calendar, no navigation. The sub-line flips to the green "DONE FOR TODAY" state the crossword card already taught users.
  • Tapping the card body (not the circle) opens the tracker from section 01.
  • "See all" leads to a Commitments screen: active ones with their bead strips, completed ones archived below with their final calendars - a quiet trophy room.
  • The count line adapts to the shape: a forty shows "Day 34 of 40"; an ongoing commitment shows the 🔥 streak instead, reusing the challenge streak pattern.
  • Missed-day nudge: if yesterday went unmarked on a consecutive commitment, this card asks first - "Did you recite yesterday?" - offering back-fill before the strict rule resets anything. Honest, but never silently punishing.
04 · Completion

Finishing a forty should feel like something

  • The completion moment lands on the day itself: the gold hero with the ziyarat's salam line, the full forty beads, and the finished calendar beneath - the same screen the user has visited for forty days, now complete.
  • The commitment archives to the Commitments screen with its calendar intact, so the record of an arba'in is kept, not discarded.
  • A badge belongs here: "The Forty" joins the existing badge set (the app already awards streak and completion badges), with smaller ones for a first commitment and a first full week.
  • Share, quietly: an optional share card with the dates and the bead strip - no scores, no leaderboard; a keepsake, not a competition.
  • Then an invitation: "Begin again, or make a new promise" - looping back to section 02.
05 · If we build it

One model, one manager, one new screen family

The calendar is new UI, but every mechanism beneath it already exists in the codebase in some form.

Data model

Commitment

  • Commitment { id, items, span, rule, startDate, reminderTime } where items: [CommitmentItem(type, refId)] reuses the content-reference idea: verse, surah, dua, ziyarat, lifeMoment...
  • span: .days(40), .untilDate, or .ongoing · rule: .consecutive or .flexible.
  • Completion log per commitment: completedDayKeys: Set<String> - the journey managers' completedDays: Set<Int> pattern, keyed by the app's standard dayKey("yyyy-MM-dd").
State & rules

CommitmentsManager

  • Day rollover via refreshForToday() on scenePhase, exactly like DailyCrosswordManager.
  • Consecutive rule: on load, if a gap is found, surface the back-fill prompt; declined means the start date resets - a pure, testable transition like nextStreak().
  • Hijri month grid and end-date math (day 40 lands on Arbaeen) from the same islamicUmmAlQura calendar the Hijri pill uses.
  • UserDefaults first, Supabase sync after, following BookmarkManager's three-step pattern.
Content & surfaces

Ziyarat Ashura ships with it

  • New content: Ziyarat Ashura full text, translation, transliteration - with a Listen option via the existing DuaListenButton/TTS path, per the house rule.
  • Surfaces: Today-tab commitment cards, the tracker screen, the create screen, the Commitments list; "Commit to this" chips on dua/ziyarat detail views.
  • Reminders through the existing notification pipeline, deep-linking into the tracker.
Rollout

House rules honored

  • What's New entry in EN/UR/AR with a new WhatsNewDestination case; no em dash in copy.
  • Premium: one active commitment free, unlimited with Premium - gold capsule, never a lock, paywall on the second "Begin".
  • Timing: shipping shortly before Muharram makes the forty land on the season; the create screen's presets can go seasonal (e.g. "Until Arbaeen") automatically.
  • Both themes ship; the mock shows Midnight Emerald, light follows the legacy card recipe.
